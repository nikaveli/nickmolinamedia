## 1. Positioning

**Who it's for:** custom home builders, remodeling contractors, design-build firms, kitchen and bath specialists, and the trades behind them (cabinet shops, tile setters, fabricators, framers). Denver metro and the Front Range.

**What it is:** a cinematic video and photography studio that films builders' real projects, during the build and at the finish, and turns one project into the film, reels, photos, and Google presence that win the next homeowner.

**The job we're hired for:** not "content." The next signed contract.

**One line for bios, intros, and the door:**
> I film finished projects for Colorado builders so their last job sells their next one.

**Taglines, strongest first:**
1. Your last job should sell your next one. (Two of the best builder agencies in adjacent markets, Copper Frame in Phoenix and Tresmot in Park City, use near-identical promises. That is validation, not a conflict: neither works Colorado.)
2. Built to be seen.
3. Real work. Filmed right.

**Trust chips, shown under the hero buttons:** One visit · Film in [7 days] · No contract · You own every file · Denver + Front Range

**The idea underneath everything:** a builder spends months on a project. The homeowner moves in. The only record is a few phone photos. Meanwhile the next homeowner is comparing three builders online and picking the one whose work they can actually see. We film the work so it keeps winning after the crew leaves.

## 2. The message ladder (every page and every reel lands on one of these, in this order)

1. **Your work is excellent.** Open with the builder's craft, never with marketing.
2. **Nobody sees enough of it.** Months of work, six phone photos.
3. **Homeowners hire what they can see.** Five- and six-figure decisions are made on visible proof.
4. **Film is the proof that moves people.** A 45-second project film does what a photo gallery can't: it walks a homeowner through the room.
5. **One visit. Months of marketing.** One shoot becomes a project film, reels, a photo set, Google profile media, and a website page.
6. **I show up myself.** One person, cinema cameras, no crew, no disruption to the homeowner, no contract.
7. **When the project is finished, it goes on the record for good.** Portfolio-grade film and photography that keep selling for years.

Voice: short, physical, concrete. Builder words (finished project, the next job, homeowners, jobsite, walkthrough). No agency words (content, branding, engagement, storytelling, omnichannel). No hype (dominate, viral, skyrocket, 10X, game-changing).

## 7. Copy rules kept from the taste skill

- One two-fragment heading per page (the central message keeps it: "Your projects take months. Your visibility shouldn't.").
- No strip of figures unless every number is Nick's own fact.
- Numbers only on steps that happen in order.
- No virtue words about the business (honest, actually, simply, quiet).
- No invented quotes, counts or reviews. The Summit quote waits until it is real.
- Buttons are a verb plus what the visitor gets.
