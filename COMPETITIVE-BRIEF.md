Read 2026-11-25 across ten sites Nick supplied. Prices deliberately not recorded; packaging structure only.

## Patterns that persuade builders

1. One line, builder words: "proof of the last one," "the work sells the work," "four to six homes in progress and no content from any of them" (Tresmot).
2. Trust chips above the fold: turnaround, no contract, you own every file, local.
3. Three recent films first, before any explanation.
4. A three-step process with real numbers (first visit within a week, videos in three days).
5. "Who we shoot for" cards naming the trades, so a remodeler sees himself.
6. Few packages. Copper Frame's are the right shapes: one visit, a multi-visit project, a monthly rhythm.
7. Terms as a plain list, not legal copy: no contract, one revision round, you own the files, no travel fee inside the metro.
8. Named builders, credited wherever the work appears. Logos mean nothing unless they are builders.
9. Progress documentation sold as its own thing (Architectural Storytelling, Copper Frame), which is exactly Nick's Document → Publish → Showcase system.
10. Trade-partner licensing: the cabinet shop and tile setter buy footage of their part.

## What to avoid

- Poetic headlines ("Light, held a little longer," "Every frame sells the story," "Show, don't tell").
- Agent-first sites where builders get one card (Listing Bees, PhotoSLC, MediaKingsmen).
- SKU sprawl: Copper Frame lists a content day, a half day, three plans, a profile-upkeep tier, three project packages, a camera, add-ons and single items on one page.
- Credit-based memberships (RKDA) that builders can't price in their head.
- Repeating the same award or testimonial on every page.
- Placeholder mockups that look like templates (Tresmot's "yourfirm.com").

## Where Nick wins

- Video-first. Most of these are still-photographers who added video. Nick leads with cinema cameras, gimbal, slider, and vertical reels cut for Instagram and Facebook.
- Progress plus finish in one relationship (the six-month problem). Only Copper Frame and Architectural Storytelling sell progress, and neither pairs it with the Google Business Profile.
- He shows up himself. One person, no crew, no account manager, no hand-off (Utah Valley Videos makes this point well).
- Front Range only, so every visit is a short drive and the same person learns the builder's work.
- He builds the website too, so film and site are made for each other (only Tresmot and RKDA offer this).

## Structural decisions for nickmolinamedia (v3)

Home: hero with trust chips → three-room scroll sequence → the problem → recent work (films) → how it works with numbers → who I film for → what one project turns into → during the build → packages (three) + Signature Website banner → why me + terms list → builder credit line → FAQ → closing.
For Builders: who it's for → packages → the Build Story process (visit one, middle visits, reveal) → objections → closing.
Packages: Project Film (one visit) · Build Story (three visits across one project) · Builder Film Plan (monthly). Website sold as the upgrade under the packages, not a fourth tier.
